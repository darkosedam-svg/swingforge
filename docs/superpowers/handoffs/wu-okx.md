# WU-OKX — a research-only venue for deeper history (`swingforge/adapters/okx/`, contract v3)

**Why it exists.** Hyperliquid serves only its newest 5,000 candles per interval: 27 months of 4H bars, 15 of them out of sample. After pooling and the ICT candidate walk the Hyperliquid tournament says "no demonstrated edge" for every ICT arm but one pattern (trailing exits under the active-hours filter, +0.18R to +0.26R on 106-111 pooled trades), and 15 months cannot tell that pattern from what a 901-trial search finds by chance. OKX's USDT-margined perpetual swaps carry 4H candles back to within weeks of listing (first 4H candle: BTC 2019-12-16, ETH 2019-12-25, SOL 2021-01-22, ARB 2023-03-23, HYPE 2025-02-21; `1Dutc` bars for BTC and ETH start 2020-01-01), so the same strategies, exits, gate and pooling can be run over the four years the design spec asks for. The owner chose OKX over Binance futures (deeper still) and Bybit on 2026-09-20.

**What it is.** A third venue, `okx`, that serves history and nothing else. `swingforge backfill|tournament|report --venue okx` work like any venue, against their own store `data/okx.duckdb`; `swingforge paper --venue okx` is refused (`_RESEARCH_ONLY_VENUES`), `OkxBars.stream` raises, there is no systemd unit for it, and the dashboard does not look for its store (`web.queries.VENUES` is unchanged, so an OKX run never displaces the Hyperliquid one as "latest tournament"). Nothing is spliced into the Hyperliquid data: the two stores, cost models and tournaments are separate, and their results are compared, not merged.

**⚠ Comparing the two venues is not like for like.** (1) *Fill resolution.* OKX serves 1H candles for the whole window, so every 4H bar replays with its four sub-bars and a stop and a target inside one bar are ordered on real data (`resolution_mode = subbars`). Hyperliquid serves about 7 months of 1H against 27 of 4H, so most of its run resolves `pessimistic` or `mixed` - the stop is assumed first. That is a one-sided advantage to OKX that has nothing to do with the strategy, and it lands on exactly the exits most sensitive to intrabar order: trailing and partial exits, which is where the one surviving Hyperliquid pattern lives. The report prints the mode per instrument; read it before comparing a config across venues. (2) *Costs.* Taker 0.05% here against 0.045%, eight-hourly funding against hourly, and funding here is mostly assumed (below). (3) *They are different books* - a USDT-margined swap and a USDC perp, with their own basis and listing dates: two price paths, not two samples of one. Ticks are not a problem, and are written down so nobody redoes the arithmetic: half a tick of slippage is `0.5 * tick / |entry - stop|` of R, under 0.07% of R for a round trip on either venue (BTC tick 1 there, 0.1 here), and `stop = wick - tick` moves a BTC stop by one part in a thousand.

**Contract v3.** `core/types.py`: `Instrument.venue` is `Literal["hyperliquid", "oanda", "okx"]`; `CONTRACT_VERSION = 3`, tag `contract-v3`. Additive - no existing value changes meaning, and nothing matched exhaustively on the old two. `tests/unit/test_types.py` pins the closed set.

**Files.** `client.py` - `OkxClient`, public REST over the standard library (no new dependency; `fetch`/`sleep`/`clock` injectable), `OkxLike` the duck-typed surface, `OkxError` for the venue's own error codes. `bars.py` - `OkxBars.history`, `inst_id`, `okx_instrument(s)`, `DEFAULT_SWAPS` (the five instruments the Hyperliquid run grades, keyed by coin - `okx:BTC` - so config ids line up across stores). `costs.py` - `OkxCosts`, `load_funding`. CLI: `Venue.OKX`, `_bar_source`, `_instruments`, `_funding`, `_build_cost_model` (shares the per-instrument dispatcher with Hyperliquid). `.importlinter`: `adapters.okx` joins the venue adapters `lab` and `web` may not import.

**What the venue does (read off the live API on 2026-09-20; the unit tests encode each).**
- `history-candles` pages **backwards**: up to 300 rows (its documentation says 100), newest first, strictly older than the `after` cursor. `history` walks back from `end` until a page reaches `start`, comes back **empty**, or makes no progress. A short page is deliberately not a stop condition (both reviews): the 300 is observed, not documented, and read as end-of-history a venue enforcing its documented 100 would hand back the newest hundred bars of every window with no error - while `backfill`'s `months=` figure, computed from the first and last bar, would still read 48. Cost: one extra request where a window opens before the listing.
- It has never omitted a candle: the 2026-09-20 preview backfill (four years, five swaps, three timeframes) holds no gap and no off-grid bar, every Daily bar equals its six 4H bars but one ETH day, and 4H equals its four 1H bars on all but 6 of ~37,000 (open/close differences, plus one SOL 4H low a hair wider than its sub-bars'; `ReplaySource` attaches sub-bars to every bar without complaint). A gap in a returned series is therefore refused (`RuntimeError`) rather than stored - nothing downstream checks contiguity. `history` sees one series at a time and `backfill` asks for a year per call, so `backfill` checks the seam between two windows itself on this venue (`_GAPLESS_VENUES`); the store as a whole is not validated. A refusal is survivable: `backfill` echoes it, carries on with the other timeframes and instruments, and exits non-zero naming every failure (three in a row end the run - that is a venue that is down or blocking, and each costs a full retry ladder of about a minute to establish, including for an HTML block page, which is retried like any non-JSON body).
- The candle still forming is served with `confirm == "0"`; it is dropped, and so is any candle whose close lies in the future whatever its flag says.
- 1H and 4H candles open on the UTC grid. The plain `1D` candle opens at **16:00 UTC** (Hong Kong midnight); Daily is asked for as `1Dutc`, and a Daily row not opening at 00:00 UTC raises rather than being stored. The contract test checks that a Daily bar equals its six 4H bars (open, close, extremes).
- Every response is `{"code", "msg", "data"}`; a non-zero `code` is an error under HTTP 200. Over-budget bursts get HTTP 429 or code 50011: the client spaces requests 0.12s apart (the budget is 20 per 2 seconds) and backs off 1, 2, 4... seconds, six times, if told off anyway. The same ladder covers what a CDN does to an ~800-request backfill - a 5xx, a reset connection, a timeout, an HTML error page (reported as `OkxError("non-json")`, not as a JSON parse error); a 4xx other than 429 is let through at once.
- Volume: `volCcy` (base coin) is stored, not the contract count.
- Sizing is in coins (`contract_multiplier = 1`), as on Hyperliquid; the venue's contract size (`ctVal`, 0.01 BTC) is an order-entry convention a price-path backtest has no use for. Tick sizes are the venue's own (`tickSz`).

**⚠ Funding is mostly an assumption.** `funding-rate-history` serves about three months and nothing older (verified: a request for a year back returns no rows). `load_funding` stores what exists (`realizedRate` preferred over `fundingRate`); `OkxCosts` charges every settlement before the first recorded one at `DEFAULT_FUNDING_RATE` = 0.01% per eight hours, the venue's baseline, longs paying. 0.01% is the venue's own `interestRate` for all five swaps, so it is the right baseline - but what matters is the error, not the charge. The charge is about 0.03R on a three-day hold (nine settlements, 0.09% of notional against a 3% stop). The error is not bounded by that: funding is capped at 0.375% per settlement for BTC and up to 1% for the alts, and a rally regime of 0.03% costs a long ~0.06R more than is charged - a quarter of the +0.2R being resolved, and one-directional, because funding is highest exactly when longs win. **Rule 6 does not cover it**: it scales the assumed charge (0.01% to 0.015%) and cannot span a real 0.05%. The reassuring half, measured on the 95 days the venue serves: mean settled rate 0.0027% to 0.0049% across the five swaps, with 0.01% the observed maximum for four of them - so in the recent regime the baseline over-charges a long (and over-credits a short). **Reading rule: an OKX result that holds by less than ~0.1R per trade is unresolved.** Fees: taker 0.05% on every fill (the regular tier; maker is 0.02%), half a tick of slippage, as on Hyperliquid.

**Not done.** No live stream, no paper, no nightly backfill unit, no dashboard card. Real funding for the whole window exists and is free: the venue's public archive serves one small zip per swap per day, `https://www.okx.com/cdn/okex/traderecords/swaprate/daily/YYYYMMDD/{instId}-swaprate-YYYY-MM-DD.zip` (checked 2026-09-20: HTTP 200, ~400 bytes, for 2023-06-15 BTC). Four years of five swaps is ~7,300 files; loading them into `funding` would retire the assumption above and is the first follow-up if an OKX result ever comes close. No run with sub-bars withheld, which would make the Hyperliquid comparison fair on fill resolution. Settlement cadence is taken as eight hours throughout; the venue can shorten it for a volatile alt, and a recorded settlement off the eight-hour grid is then applied from the next grid boundary on.

## Runs (2026-09-20, `main` at `27b139f`, seed 0)

**Backfill.** `backfill --venue okx --years 4`: 4.5 minutes, clean exit. BTC, ETH and SOL hold 47 months (35,039 1H / 8,759 4H / 1,459 Daily bars each), ARB 41, HYPE 18. No gap and no off-grid bar in any timeframe; every Daily bar equals its six 4H bars but one ETH day; `ReplaySource` attaches sub-bars to every 4H bar. Some 283 funding settlements per swap (2026-06-17 on), everything older at the baseline.

**Four-year tournament, `tournament:okx:20260920`.** 908 trials (757 per instrument and view, 151 universe), `trial_sr_variance` 0.0109, **0 passed**. Out of sample is 36 months for the three majors. Pooled across the five swaps, averaged over the 16 exits:

| entry | session | pooled n_oos | avg exp_oos (R) | rows clearing rule 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ict | none | 325-418 | -0.001 | 16/16 |
| ict | london_ny | 258-301 | -0.001 | 16/16 |
| ict | active | 185-209 | -0.012 | 16/16 |
| baseline | none | 215-251 | -0.000 | 16/16 |
| zones | none | 36-37 | -0.29 | 0/16 |

With three to four hundred pooled trades a row, ICT's out-of-sample expectancy is zero in every arm - indistinguishable from the random baseline it is matched against. Per instrument with no filter (16-exit average): BTC +0.12R, ETH +0.03R, SOL +0.03R, ARB -0.08R, HYPE -0.49R on 21 trades. 117 single-instrument ICT rows clear rule 1 on their own; none passes. The best row anywhere is `ict|structure_2|london_ny|okx:BTC` - 66 trades, +0.85R out of sample against +0.92R in sample, bootstrap 5th percentile +0.005R, ahead of the baseline and of buy-and-hold - and it fails rule 2 with a deflated-Sharpe probability of 0.024 against 0.95: one such row is what 908 trials produce by chance, and its margin over zero at the 5th percentile is far inside the ~0.1R funding uncertainty above. It is a lead, not a result.

**The Hyperliquid pattern does not survive four years.** Hyperliquid's last run left one pattern standing: all four trailing exits positive under the active-hours filter (+0.18R to +0.26R on 106-111 pooled trades). Over 36 months out of sample on OKX the same four rows read +0.08R, +0.12R, **-0.13R and -0.13R** on 185-202 trades, none with a bootstrap 5th percentile above -0.13R; and the arm that was worst on Hyperliquid (trailing exits, no filter, -0.33R to -0.37R) reads +0.15R and +0.17R here for two of its exits and -0.13R for the other two. Signs that flip with the window are what noise looks like.

**Same window, two venues (`tournament:okx:20260920-hlwindow`, `--start 2024-05-26T16:00 --end 2026-09-07T00:00`).** To separate period from venue, OKX was re-run on exactly Hyperliquid's 27 months (901 trials, 0 passed) and compared with `tournament:hyperliquid:20260920`:

| pooled, 16-exit average | Hyperliquid | OKX, same window |
|---|---|---|
| ict, none | -0.193R on 204-258 trades | -0.178R on 193-240 |
| ict, london_ny | -0.073R on 152-181 | -0.074R on 143-167 |
| ict, active | +0.071R on 106-119 | +0.060R on 103-116 |
| zones, none | -0.330R on 19-20 | -0.404R on 16 |

1. *The venues agree.* Two independent data sources, two adapters, one engine: per-instrument trade counts within about 10% (12% at worst), zones within 0.01R on most rows (0.11R at worst, on three to five trades), and every ICT exit with a fixed stop and target - the six ATR exits, the three `fixed_r` and `structure_2` - within 0.04R when the four common instruments are pooled, nine of the ten within 0.02R. That cross-validates both adapters, and says the four-year result above is about the period, not about OKX: on Hyperliquid's own window OKX shows the same trailing-under-active-hours pattern (+0.11R to +0.35R, all four positive). The pattern is real for those 15 months and absent before them.
2. *Fill resolution is worth +0.08R to +0.17R on a trailing or partial exit.* The one systematic difference: `trail_1_1` and `trail_2_1` read +0.17R better on OKX than on Hyperliquid over the same trades, `trail_1_2`, `trail_2_2` and the two partial exits +0.08R, and every exit with a fixed stop and target within 0.04R. OKX replays `subbars` throughout; Hyperliquid reports `mixed` for every instrument because it serves only ~7 months of 1H candles, so for most of its history a stop and a moving target inside one 4H bar are resolved stop-first. Hyperliquid's trailing and partial results are therefore pessimistic by about that much, by construction of the data it serves, and OKX's are the fairer reading of those exits - which did not rescue them.

**Where this leaves the question.** On crypto perps at 4H, over four years and five instruments, with pooling and the candidate walk, ICT as specified has no demonstrated out-of-sample edge (pooled expectancy 0.00R on ~300 trades a row), zones has a negative one on a small sample, and the statistical gate has done what it was built to do twice over - first by refusing thin samples, then by refusing a period-specific pattern once the sample existed to test it. What remains open is outside this venue: the FX half (OANDA credentials), and real funding for OKX if the BTC `structure_2` lead is ever pursued.
