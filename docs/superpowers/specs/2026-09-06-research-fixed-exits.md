# Fixed SL/TP Swing Entries ("Set and Forget"): An Evidence-First Assessment for an ICT/SMC Algo Trader

## TL;DR
- **Fixed SL/TP as a standalone edge: low probability.** The edge lives in the entry and the regime, not the exit rule. A fixed pip/fixed-R exit that ignores volatility, session, and structure is dominated in most tested conditions by ATR-scaled and structure-based exits — but it is a legitimate, hard-to-beat *baseline* and a rational choice under prop-firm constraints and for high-win-rate mean-reversion. Probability it beats a well-built adaptive exit on your ICT setups out-of-sample: low (~20–30%).
- **The academic literature is unanimous on the mechanism:** stops (and by symmetry, fixed exits) subtract expected return under random-walk/mean-reverting price processes and add value only under momentum/positive autocorrelation (Kaminski & Lo 2014; Han/Zhou/Zhu 2016; Lei & Li 2009). Their consistent finding is that fixed stops help **risk-adjusted** metrics (drawdown, Sharpe) more than raw return.
- **Alex G's "Set and Forget" is a beginner-marketed supply/demand + support-resistance swing method (Daily/Weekly → 4H, ~1:3 R:R, 1–2% risk, pending orders then walk away).** It is pedagogically reasonable but carries no demonstrated edge; its headline "$100 → $1,000,000" claim is unverified and judged implausible by independent watchdogs. Treat it as a discipline framework, not a source of alpha.

## Key Findings

**1. The exit rule is a second-order effect; entry and regime dominate.** The most rigorous public ICT backtest (StatOasis, Sept 2026, 648 backtests on SPY/QQQ/DIA/IWM daily bars) held exits constant to isolate entry quality and found no ICT concept produced a statistically significant forward-return edge — the best, order blocks on SPY, reached only t=+1.22 (below the significance bar of 2), and 0 of 648 variants beat buy-and-hold. This is a general lesson: obsessing over fixed-vs-adaptive exits while the entry has no measured edge is misallocated effort.

**2. Fixed exits are theoretically dominated except under momentum.** Kaminski & Lo prove stop-loss rules always reduce expected return under a random walk and add value only under momentum/regime-switching. Han, Zhou & Zhu show a 10% stop on momentum portfolios cut worst monthly loss from −49.79% to −11.36% and more than doubled the Sharpe. Lei & Li find fixed and trailing stops on individual stocks "neither reduce nor increase" returns vs buy-and-hold but reduce risk. Consensus: **fixed exits are a risk-management tool, not a return-generation tool.**

**3. ATR-scaled stops structurally dominate fixed-pip stops on FX.** A fixed 20-pip stop means something completely different on EUR/USD (low ATR) vs GBP/JPY (high ATR) and during a quiet London morning vs an NFP release. Multiple quant backtests report ATR-based stops improve risk-adjusted returns and reduce drawdown vs fixed-pip stops. The structural flaw of fixed pips is that it applies one distance to heterogeneous volatility; ATR normalization keeps dollar risk constant and places the stop outside normal noise.

**4. Broker data supports R:R *discipline*, not fixed R:R as edge.** DailyFX/FXCM's study of 43M+ real trades found traders win >50% of the time on most pairs yet lose money because average losses exceed average wins. On EUR/USD, FXCM clients closed trades at a profit 61% of the time, yet the average losing trade (83 pips) was far larger than the average winner (48 pips) — "traders lost 70% more on their losing trades than they won on winning trades" (DailyFX/FXCM *Traits of Successful Traders*, Q2 2014–Q1 2015). In the same firm's data, 52% of accounts using ≥1:1 reward:risk turned a net profit vs only 20% of those under 1:1 — adherents were over 2.5x more likely to profit. Retail loss rates (ESMA product-intervention decision, 23 March 2018: "74–89% of retail accounts typically lose money … with average losses per client ranging from €1,600 to €29,000") reflect behavioral failure, not the failure of fixed targets. Fixed SL/TP's real value for most humans is that it *removes management discretion* — which is exactly where retail traders destroy edge.

**5. "Set and forget" is compatible with structure-based targets — and better for it.** Nothing about fixed-at-entry exits requires *pip-based* levels. The correct ICT/SMC implementation is fixed-at-entry but *structure-derived*: stop beyond the liquidity sweep/order block invalidation, target at the opposing liquidity pool / draw on liquidity / FVG fill. This keeps the behavioral benefit of no-management while discarding the volatility-blindness of fixed pips.

## Details

### What "fixed SL/TP swing entries" is as a strategy class
Pre-defined SL and TP set at entry, no active management, held on daily/4H over days. Two sub-variants matter and are routinely conflated:
- **Fixed-pip / fixed-percentage** exits: a constant distance (e.g., 30 pips, 1%) regardless of instrument or volatility. This is the structurally flawed version.
- **Fixed-R** exits: SL at a logical level, TP at a constant multiple (1:2, 1:3). Less flawed, because the R distance can itself be volatility- or structure-derived; only the *ratio* is fixed.

The critique below applies most harshly to fixed-pip; fixed-R with a structure-based stop is defensible.

### Alex G "Set and Forget" — what it reportedly is, and source reliability
Alex Gonzalez (fxalexg / FX Alex G, b. 2000, Miami), founder of Swing Trading Lab, teaches a course called "Set and Forget" (officially ~$1,499–$1,500; widely pirated/resold for $19–$30). Reconstructed mechanics from public sources:
- **Entry:** "Areas of Interest" (AOI) = key support/resistance and supply/demand zones, aligned with psychological/round numbers ("500"/"000") as institutional-order confluence. Naked price-action charts, no indicators. Pending limit/stop orders placed at the zone.
- **Timeframes:** Top-down — Weekly/Daily for structure/bias, 4H for execution. Swing horizon (days).
- **R:R and management:** Risk 1–2% per trade, target minimum 1:3; set SL and TP at entry and close the platform — literal set-and-forget, no management.
- **Instruments:** Forex-centric, scanning 20+ pairs weekly for setups; gold and other markets referenced by resellers (loosely sourced).

**Source reliability:** The mechanics come primarily from a course-reseller summary (semi-reliable for describing content, not for endorsement) and his own promo (unreliable). Notably, **no substantive independent Reddit r/Forex or ForexFactory strategy dissection surfaced** — a gap worth flagging. Independent watchdogs are uniformly skeptical: WikiFX characterizes the "$100 → $1M" claim (he claims $88,000 in 27 days, ~$1M in ~2 months, 8,800% in 21 days, 70% win rate) as almost certainly fabricated, notes he never shows live broker statements, may use TradingView market-replay to fake live calls, and once posted a $7,988 "gain" on a trade that should have lost. ### Academic and empirical evidence on fixed vs adaptive exits
- **Kaminski & Lo (2014, *Journal of Financial Markets*):** Under the Random Walk Hypothesis, simple stop-loss rules always decrease expected return; empirically, a stop-loss overlay on a US long-term-bond buy-and-hold (monthly data 1950–2004) "improved the return over the buy-and-hold strategy by 50–100 basis points" during stop-out periods, driven by momentum/regime-switching (flights-to-quality). At short sampling frequencies stops add no value; at longer intervals they can raise Sharpe. The value depends on the return-generating process.
- **Han, Zhou & Zhu (2016), "Taming Momentum Crashes":** With data from Jan 1926–Dec 2013, a 10% stop cut equal-weighted momentum's worst monthly loss from −49.79% to −11.36% (value-weighted −64.97% to −23.28%) while "the Sharpe ratios are more than doubled." The benefit is downside-tail truncation, not return enhancement.
- **Lei & Li (2009), "The Value of Stop Loss Strategies":** On individual US stocks with transaction costs, fixed and trailing stops "neither reduce nor increase" losses vs buy-and-hold once you extend returns to future paths; one trailing mechanism reduced risk. Value comes from **risk reduction, not return improvement.**
- **Trailing vs fixed vs time vs ATR:** Trailing stops outperform fixed TP in *trending* regimes (they capture open-ended moves; fixed TP caps the winner) but bleed via premature exits and whipsaw in *choppy/mean-reverting* regimes. Fixed TP wins for mean reversion where the expected move is small and bounded. ATR/volatility-scaled exits generally win on risk-adjusted metrics across regimes because they adapt the distance. Time-based exits are a useful secondary overlay (exit if the thesis hasn't played out in N bars), consistent with the "edge decays with holding period" finding in event-driven work.

### The structural critique
Fixed-pip/fixed-R exits that ignore volatility, session, and structure are dominated by:
- **(a) ATR-scaled stops** — keep dollar risk constant across heterogeneous-volatility pairs; place the stop outside normal noise; enable volatility-targeted position sizing (risk% ÷ ATR-distance).
- **(b) Structure-based stops** — below the swing low / order-block invalidation / beyond the sweep; the stop sits where the trade thesis is actually wrong, not at an arbitrary distance.
- **(c) Partial-profit / breakeven management** — scale out at 1R, trail the runner; captures the asymmetric tail that full-exit-at-fixed-TP structurally forfeits.

**When fixed SL/TP is genuinely fine:** (1) as a simple, honest baseline every adaptive method must beat; (2) for prop-firm rule compliance (below); (3) for high-win-rate mean-reversion setups with a small bounded target where trailing adds nothing; (4) when the trader's discretionary management demonstrably destroys expectancy (the majority retail case) — here the behavioral gain from removing management can exceed the theoretical loss from a suboptimal exit.

### ICT/SMC exit logic and backtest evidence
ICT-native exits are structure-derived: stop beyond the liquidity sweep or order-block mitigation; target the opposing liquidity pool / draw on liquidity / FVG fill. "Set and forget" is fully compatible with this — it's fixed-at-entry but structure-anchored, not pip-anchored. On the evidence: mechanical ICT concepts show no significant standalone forward edge on daily bars (StatOasis: order blocks the strongest, t=+1.22, 0/648 beat buy-and-hold; OTE/Fibonacci band never beat random on SPY; FVG 5-day forward edge −0.005%). Retail/vendor SMC backtests claiming 55–75% win rates exist but are lower-reliability (self-interested, often selling indicators/courses, rarely with baselines). The honest read: **any ICT edge, if it exists, lives in discretion/intraday execution that is not backtestable from published rules — so you cannot assume a structure-based exit inherits an edge the entry never demonstrated.**

### Prop-firm angle
Fixed SL/TP is heavily taught in the challenge ecosystem because the rules make bounded, predictable per-trade risk mandatory: daily loss limits (typically 4–5%), max/trailing drawdown (5–10%), and consistency rules (single-day profit capped at ~30–50% of total). Static, pre-defined risk lets you pre-compute lot sizes, never breach a daily limit via slippage, and avoid the tail outcomes that trailing/scaling can produce. The constraint shapes exit design toward fixed-R with conservative sizing (0.25–1% per trade). Note this is an *artifact of the rules*, not evidence the exit is optimal for expectancy — and firms differ (some now offer static drawdown / no-consistency-rule accounts better suited to swing/runner styles).

### Practical takeaways for a Python algo trader
- **Test protocol:** MAE/MFE excursion analysis first (winners' MAE distribution sets the minimum non-noise stop; MFE distribution sets realistic TP and reveals exit-efficiency = captured/MFE — a widen-the-stop decision is statistically justified only when ~30%+ of winners dipped past the current stop before recovering). Then an exit-grid sweep (SL multiple × TP multiple, plus trailing and time exits) under walk-forward, not a single in-sample optimum. Split by regime (trend vs range via ADX/Hurst/realized-vol) — the fixed-vs-adaptive answer flips by regime.
- **Metrics to compare:** expectancy (R), Sharpe, MAR/Calmar, profit factor, tail ratio, time-in-market, and drawdown — not win rate (structure-based stops routinely trade win rate for larger average winners and higher profit factor). Always include buy-and-hold and a frequency-matched random-entry baseline.
- **Pitfalls:** look-ahead in TP fills (don't assume intrabar TP fills before SL when both are touched in one bar — model the pessimistic order or use tick data); spread/slippage on stops (stops become market orders; exotic/off-hours spread can eat 30–50% of a tight ATR stop; add spread to the stop distance); overfitting the R:R grid (a "best" 1:2.7 found in-sample is usually noise — prefer robust plateaus, penalize parameter sensitivity); survivorship/regime bias (a fixed stop that "wins" on a trending backtest sample may fail out-of-sample).

## Recommendations
1. **Use fixed-R with structure-derived stops as your baseline, and require every adaptive variant to beat it out-of-sample.** Don't deploy adaptive exits that only win in-sample. Benchmark: adaptive must improve MAR or Sharpe by a margin that survives walk-forward and cost stress.
2. **Replace fixed-pip with ATR-normalized distances immediately** — this is the single highest-confidence upgrade and is nearly free. Keep the R-ratio fixed if you like; make the R-distance volatility/structure-based.
3. **Run MAE/MFE on your existing ICT trade log before touching exit code.** If winners' median MAE exceeds your stop, you are being stopped out of winners by noise; if exit efficiency <60%, your fixed TP is leaving the tail on the table — test partial-scale + trail on the runner.
4. **Regime-gate the exit:** fixed TP for mean-reversion setups (sweep-into-range reversals), trailing/partial for trend-continuation setups (MSS + displacement into open liquidity). One exit rule for both regimes is the mistake.
5. **For prop evaluation, run a separate fixed-R, low-sizing config** tuned to the specific firm's drawdown/consistency mechanics — treat it as a constraint-satisfaction problem distinct from your expectancy-max config. Prefer firms with static drawdown / no consistency rule if you trade runners.
6. **Do not pay for "Set and Forget" expecting edge.** The framework (top-down, zones, fixed risk, no management) is reconstructable free; the pedagogy has behavioral value but no demonstrated alpha, and the performance claims are unverified.

**Probability-style judgments:**
- Fixed SL/TP as a standalone edge: **low (~15–25%)**.
- Fixed SL/TP as a baseline worth beating / behavioral safeguard: **high value, high confidence**.
- Fixed-pip (volatility-blind) beating ATR-normalized on heterogeneous FX pairs OOS: **low (~15%)**.
- Structure-based/partial management beating full-fixed-TP on trend-continuation ICT setups: **moderate-to-high (~60–70%)**.
- Alex G's specific method delivering the marketed returns: **very low (~1–2%)**.

## Caveats
- **Source tiers, explicitly:** Peer-reviewed (Kaminski & Lo; Han/Zhou/Zhu; Lei & Li) is strongest but mostly equities/portfolio-level, not FX-swing-specific — extrapolate the *mechanism* (momentum vs mean-reversion determines stop value), not the exact numbers. StatOasis is a high-quality independent backtest but daily-bars/index-ETF/long-only and explicitly cannot test intraday discretionary ICT. Broker stats (DailyFX/FXCM, ESMA) are large-sample and reliable for behavior but come from conflicted parties (brokers profit from volume). ATR/exit backtests from quant blogs vary widely in rigor and several are vendor content — treat specific percentage improvements as illustrative, not established. Alex G specifics rest on reseller summaries and skeptical watchdogs; the absence of independent forum dissection is itself a caveat.
- **Regime dependence is the crux:** every "fixed vs adaptive" claim is conditional on trend vs mean-reversion. Your ICT setups mix both (sweeps = mean-reversion entries; MSS+displacement = momentum entries), so a single global exit verdict is wrong by construction — split it.
- **Costs invert conclusions:** frictionless backtests flatter active exits; realistic spread/slippage on FX and Hyperliquid perps (funding, taker fees, thin off-hours books) can erase a trailing-stop advantage that whipsaws in and out.
- **Nothing here validates ICT/SMC entry edge itself** — the exit question is downstream of an entry edge you must establish independently against baselines.
